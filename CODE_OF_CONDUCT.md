# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Velmora to be a good experience for everyone, whatever their age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We will act and talk in ways that keep this an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## Our standards

Things that help:

- being kind and patient with other people
- respecting opinions, viewpoints and experiences that differ from yours
- giving useful feedback and taking it gracefully
- owning your mistakes, apologising to the people they affected, and learning from them
- focusing on what's best for the project and the people who use it

Things we won't accept:

- sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- public or private harassment
- publishing someone's private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
- anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Responsibilities

The maintainers clarify and enforce these standards. They may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that don't follow this Code of Conduct, and will explain moderation decisions when that's appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and the repository itself) and wherever someone officially represents the project in public, such as posting from a project account.

## Reporting

Report abusive, harassing or otherwise unacceptable behaviour to the maintainers at **ashfaqizop@gmail.com**. Every report is reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly, and the reporter's privacy is respected.

## Enforcement

The maintainers decide on the consequences of a breach using these guidelines.

### 1. Correction

Behaviour: inappropriate language or other behaviour that is unprofessional or unwelcome.

Consequence: a private, written warning that explains why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

Behaviour: a single incident or a series of actions that breach this code.

Consequence: a warning that sets out what happens if it continues. For a set period, no contact with the people involved, including unsolicited contact with the maintainers. This covers project spaces and outside channels such as social media. Breaking these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

Behaviour: a serious breach, including sustained inappropriate behaviour.

Consequence: a temporary ban from any interaction or public communication with the community. No public or private contact with the people involved is allowed during that time. Breaking these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

Behaviour: a pattern of breaches, including sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of groups of people.

Consequence: a permanent ban from any public interaction with the community.
